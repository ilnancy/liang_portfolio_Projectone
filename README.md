## Project Overview

For project 3, the major choices that I made in revising my site were to primarily focus on the navigation bar, the flex boxes, margins and spacing, and more stylizing the pages with consistency and legibility. Across all pages, I incorporated the purple box border. I changed all words to lowercase and added 300 words to my bio for the home page. I added a welcome/intro text as well. For my GIF, I used Adobe Illustrator and used the image trace tool, and changed the color and texture. I originally used the exact images that I took and made the GIF animation, but I wanted to add a little texture to the animation. 
The navigation bar was the most fun and exhilarating change for the site. I used Adobe Illustrator to create the four icons by utilizing simple shapes. For the color, I wanted a color that is clear to see. I went for a buttery yellow pastel color (hex code: yellow #feec9e). I got feedback that adding the text and not just the icons would be more user-friendly or accessible. It was a struggle to align the icons to the text. I added margins as well, so it looks more cleaner. The user can either click on the icons or the text to go to the next site. 
For the home page, I added the cat walking animation to match the cat theme. I did want to add it to the bottom, but it looked a bit off. I also wanted to add it to each page, but it might be too busy. I thought the home page would be good enough. I added text-indent to the bio paragraph text.
For the inspiration page, I chose this site to be the responsive page. However, I am not sure why it wasn’t working. I changed px to %, but then for the purple border for the Spotify container, it was not showing up when changed to percentage. What I added is the glowing effect animation for “i am inspired by”. 
For the my top 5’s page, I could not figure out how to have the list on top of the images or the list next to the images without overlapping. I changed it into rows instead of columns. I also added the purple borders to the images.
Lastly, for the hobby page, the feedback was to add a description and incorporate a carousel feature for the images. I was trying multiple versions, and the design of the carousel that caught my attention was the designer Christian Schaefer's version. I really liked the numbers and the flow of clicking to the next image. The issue that I had was whenever you do click on the numbers, the page slightly goes down. I was going to take out the same images (with the hover effect) that I used for the carousel, but I wanted to keep them to add links to the food places. I again added the purple border for consistency.
My design, coding, and visual thinking have developed over the semester through the things that I learned from both taking the course then and now. My takeaways would be legibility, hierarchy, margins, user-friendly, organization, text-align to not be centered, picking the right font for clear readability (make sure to check font-weight: normal and not have the text be bold), and overall to at least try your best. This class challenged me to push further to add my learned skills to my assignments. I thought more about legibility because if it isn’t user-friendly and easily accessible/easy to navigate, then the site will be confusing. There was an assignment where we had to write what we learned from the website regarding how to indicate if a website is user-friendly. It was a great reference when creating my site. I was careful in coding my site, making sure that it was fairly easy to navigate. I made sure that the text is easy to read, making it a larger font size. As well as the images being a decent size to see. For the top 5’s page, I did have issues with the image resolution being blurry(top five favorite pictures of my cats), but other than that, I made sure that the other pictures are clear and high resolution.
